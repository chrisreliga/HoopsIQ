The verdict

C+

Star power they can't build around, and no money left to fix it.

Phoenix is paying championship prices for a play-in roster. Booker's supermax eats a third of the payroll on its own, and once you stack Green's extension on top of it, 62% of the books are tied to three players who have never won a series together. Brooks is the one honest deal on the sheet. The problem isn't that they're expensive — plenty of good teams are expensive. The problem is they're expensive and stuck: no cap space, a tax bill on the way, and no obvious way to move the money without attaching something they'd rather keep.

The Deep Dive

Three questions that decide the next four years

The Books What the money says

Being over the cap isn't unusual — most contenders are. Being over the second apron is different. It's the line the league drew to stop teams from buying their way out of mistakes, and Phoenix is $17.1M past it. In plain terms: they can't sign anybody good, they can't merge two salaries into one bigger one in a trade, and one of their future draft picks gets locked away as a penalty.

So the roster you see is close to the roster they're stuck with. The only real currency they have left is Nick Richards' expiring deal and whatever a team is willing to give up for Jalen Green.

No mid-level exception No sign-and-trade No salary aggregation 2033 first-rounder frozen
The Core What the talent says

Booker, Green and Brooks have never won a playoff series together, and last spring they got swept. That's the whole sample. The scoring is real — this team finished 9th in offense — but the shot quality tells on them: a lot of long twos, a lot of late-clock isolation, not much rim pressure that doesn't come from a broken play.

The encouraging part is underneath. Mark Williams, Ryan Dunn, Khaman Maluach and Rasheer Fleming are all 24 or younger on contracts totaling less than what Green makes by himself. If this team gets better, it probably gets better from the bottom up, not the top down.

9th in offense 22nd in defense 28th in rim frequency Swept in round 1
The Test What has to happen

One playoff series. That's it. Not a title, not a conference finals — one series won with Booker as the best player on the floor. Win it, and the supermax looks like continuity instead of a trap, Brooks looks like a steal, and the young players get another year to grow behind a team that matters.

Lose it, and the clock on Jalen Green's option becomes the loudest thing in the building. Phoenix has until the summer of 2028 to find out, because that's the first offseason the money actually gives them a choice.
